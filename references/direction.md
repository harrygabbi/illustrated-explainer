# Visual and writing direction

## Hook and pacing

Open on a picture the viewer can understand immediately. Use a specific detail to introduce a contradiction or stake. In a time-travel story, a phone can keep the same clock time while its date changes. In a survival story, the first danger can belong to the mother while the viewer waits to be born.

Use the next scenes to show the question and its consequence. Follow an approved script where fidelity matters. For an original script, shorten repeated setup and move between scenes, comparisons, and evidence. At 195–215 words per minute, a 1,000-word read takes roughly five minutes before added holds. Treat that range as a starting point for a brisk read and leave space for an unfamiliar number or a reveal. A silent review has its own reading pace.

Begin by budgeting one new composition or meaningful annotation every three to five seconds during the hook. Let a detailed explanation hold when the narration needs time to read it. Cutaways can use a white field, one object or diagram, and a short handwritten label. Use a wider scene to show the setting and a closer shot to show the character's reaction.

## Illustration

Build depth within the still. An inhabited camp can have a foreground of grass and stones, a ring of uneven rocks around the fire, baskets and hides beside the figures, and a river and trees behind them. Use a clear silhouette and facial expression. Let line weight and a small light source establish what matters.

The original ink study used this starting palette. Adjust it by location, subject, and time of day.

| Role | Color |
| --- | --- |
| Paper and faces | `#FFFFFF` |
| Dark ink | `#171410` |
| Indoor cream | `#F3E0C3` |
| Wood | `#BC9A78` |
| Daylight blue | `#C7E5EE` |
| Leaf green | `#75A75D` |
| Warning red | `#D04738` |
| Sunset coral | `#F5B8A0` |
| Ground ochre | `#AF8E63` |

For a cover, use the strongest emotion from the episode. Put a short headline where it stays legible against the illustration and leave room for an expressive face, the setting, and a clue. A family gathered by a warm campfire with eyes visible beyond the light can suggest a survival story at phone size.
